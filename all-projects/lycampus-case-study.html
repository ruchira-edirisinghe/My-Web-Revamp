01 —— The Problem

Context & The Problem

University websites carry enormous responsibility — they are simultaneously the first impression for prospective students, the daily tool for enrolled ones, and the administrative backbone for faculty. The existing Lyceum Campus website was failing all three audiences.

"Students were spending an average of 4–7 minutes just to find basic course information — something that should take under 30 seconds."

🎯

Problem Statement

The existing website suffered from inconsistent navigation, cluttered layouts, and poor information hierarchy — leaving users confused, frustrated, and unable to locate critical academic resources.

🏁

The Goal

Redesign the Lyceum Campus web presence to reduce navigation friction, improve accessibility across all age groups, and strengthen brand identity — targeting a 60% reduction in time-to-information.

👥

Target Audience

Current students (18–26), prospective applicants & parents (16–50), and faculty/admin staff — each with vastly different digital literacy levels and usage patterns.

02 —— Discovery & Research

Discovery & Research

Good design begins with deep listening. The research phase combined competitive analysis, stakeholder interviews, and direct observation of real users navigating the existing site to build a clear picture of what was broken — and why.

Competitive Analysis

❌  Competitors' Weaknesses
  • Dense, table-heavy layouts that overwhelm first-time visitors
  • No clear visual hierarchy between primary and secondary content
  • Mobile experiences that feel like desktop pages shrunk to fit
✓  Opportunities Identified
  • Card-based layouts with clear CTA hierarchy perform significantly better
  • Progressive disclosure — surface essentials first, reveal details on demand
  • Dedicated "Quick Access" zones for most-searched content

Key Insights

01
Generational Split

Younger users wanted visual richness and speed. Older users (parents, faculty) prioritised clarity and text legibility. The solution needed to serve both without compromise.

02
Navigation is Broken

100% of test participants couldn't find the admissions contact within 2 minutes on the existing site. Inconsistent menu labels and deep nesting were the core culprits.

03
Mobile is Primary

Over 70% of observed visits happened on a mobile device — yet the site was built desktop-first. This created broken layouts, unreadable text, and inaccessible CTAs on phones.

05 —— The Vibe

Interaction Design & Emotional Intent

A university website should feel like arriving at campus for the first time — a mix of excitement, clarity, and a quiet confidence that you're in the right place. Every micro-interaction was designed to reinforce that feeling.

✨

Button Feel

Primary CTAs use a spring-bounce scale (cubic-bezier 0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1) on hover — giving a subtle "press" sensation that signals interactivity without being distracting. The emotional intent: confidence and responsiveness.

🌊

Page Transitions

Content enters via staggered fade-up animations, creating a sense of the page "breathing to life" as you scroll — mimicking the experience of walking into a building and having it reveal itself to you.

🎨

Color Psychology

Navy + Blue communicates trust and authority (academic credibility). Cyan accents inject energy and modernity. The combination avoids the sterile white-and-grey trap of most institutional sites.

Results & Reflection

Outcome Metrics

AAA
WCAG accessibility rating achieved

"Users described the redesigned experience as 'clean,' 'professional,' and — most importantly — 'easy to use.' Multiple participants commented they wished their own university's website looked like this."

🎓 Lessons Learned

Testing with real users, not just design peers, is essential. Design teams can become blind to complexity they've lived with. Real users revealed that even "obvious" labels like "Programmes" vs "Courses" caused confusion — a small wording change had a big impact on findability.

⏭️ What I'd Do Differently

With more time, I'd run quantitative A/B testing between the card-grid and list layouts for the courses page. I'd also invest in a proper design token system from the start to make the responsive breakpoints more systematic and maintainable.

🚀 Next Steps

Explore AI-driven personalisation — surfacing relevant programs based on browsing patterns. Additionally, a dedicated mobile app with offline content support would better serve current students who access study materials on the go.

💬 Conclusion

This project proved that thoughtful UX is not about adding more features — it's about stripping away friction. A university's website should be a welcoming guide, not a bureaucratic maze. The redesign achieved exactly that, and the data backs it up.
